# Reminder: validate whether Clay can be sold

Before investing in a public-product overhaul, test whether Clay solves a painful problem for anyone besides its current owner-user.

## Validation checkpoint

Invite three to five developers who already use coding agents to run real repository work in Clay without a guided demo. Observe their existing workflow first, then watch them use Clay for at least one meaningful task.

Treat these as evidence that Clay may be sellable:

- They return and use it again without being prompted.
- They delegate work that matters, not a disposable demo task.
- Clay replaces a workflow involving multiple terminals, chats, scripts, or manual status tracking.
- They notice or complain when Clay is unavailable or unreliable.
- At least one person offers to pay, asks for a paid plan, or commits meaningful setup and migration effort.

Compliments, feature requests, stars, and “I would use this” are interest, not demand.

## Decision rule

Do not optimize pricing, onboarding, hosting, or public-scale architecture until at least two external developers voluntarily return to Clay for real work. If nobody returns, narrow the product to the one workflow they found most valuable or keep it intentionally personal.
